Independent fashion labels

A business manager for the independent fashion label whose founder does everything

In a small label the designer is also the production manager, the sales team, the photographer’s assistant and the person answering customer email at midnight. We take the business around the collection off that list and work as part of your studio.

The season is always closer than it looks

A fashion label runs on dates it does not control. Fabric arrives late. Samples come back wrong. The shoot is moved. Through all of it the selling calendar stays fixed, and buyers, editors and customers expect the label to appear on time and look composed.

In a small label, the founder absorbs every delay personally. By the time the collection is finished, there is nothing left for the work that sells it. The line sheet is built the night before an appointment. The press note is two sentences. The website still shows last season on the first screen, and the social account has been silent for six weeks because every hour went into production.

The clothes are good. What is missing is someone whose job is everything around the clothes.

What we take off the founder’s desk

We work as an outsourced business manager inside your studio. The list is agreed in writing, and for a label it can include:

  • A weekly conversation with the founder, short and written up, so decisions stick.
  • A season calendar that works backward from the dates that cannot move: market appointments, the shoot, the launch, the shipping window.
  • Keeping the selling tools current: line sheets, lookbook copy, the brand summary and the wholesale terms page.
  • Coordination with the photographer, the stylist, the web developer and the showroom or sales agent if you have one.
  • Follow-up after appointments and enquiries, so a buyer who showed interest hears from you within the week.
  • A monthly report in plain words on what went out and what came back.

We do not design, source or produce, and we do not keep your books. We work alongside your accountant, your production partners and your sales agent.

Marketing: a point of view, said plainly

A label has to communicate a point of view before a customer can want to be part of it. That is harder than it sounds, because the founder is so close to the work that the idea behind it feels too obvious to say out loud.

We begin with three questions. Who are you? What are you about? Why should a buyer or a customer choose this label? We work through them with you until the answers fit on one page and sound like you.

Everything else follows from that page:

  • The brand story. Short, medium and long versions, so the label reads the same on a hangtag, on the about page and in a buyer’s deck.
  • Product language. Descriptions that tell a customer what a piece is made of, how it fits and why it was made that way.
  • The website. The current collection first. Clear sizing, shipping and returns. A wholesale enquiry route that is easy to find.
  • Email. A simple schedule for launches, restocks and the occasional letter from the founder. The list is yours, and it is the one audience no platform can take away.
  • Collection copy. One paragraph on the idea behind each season, written before the shoot so the photographs and the words agree.

PR: giving an editor a reason

Editors and stylists receive more pitches than they can open. A label earns attention by making their job easy. That means a clear angle, good images, accurate credits and samples that arrive when promised.

We prepare the materials: the collection note, the founder biography, the image selection with captions and credits, and the fact sheet with prices and stockists. We build a short, considered list of editors, stylists and writers whose work suits the label. We write each pitch for the person receiving it, and we follow up politely.

We also keep the sample log, so you know which pieces are out, with whom, and when they are due back.

Editorial placement is the editor’s decision and we will never promise it. We can promise that the label shows up prepared.

Social media: the studio is the story

Followers of an independent label want to see how the clothes come to exist: the sketch, the fabric table, the fitting, the mistake that became the best piece in the collection. This is content only you have.

We make it manageable:

  • A shared folder for phone photos and clips from the studio.
  • A monthly calendar you approve once, mixing process, product, people wearing the pieces and launch dates.
  • Captions in the founder’s voice.
  • Messages and comments watched, with customer questions answered the same day.

If you send pieces to creators or pay for posts, those relationships need to be disclosed clearly in the post. We build that into the brief so nobody has to remember it later.

Honest claims

Fashion marketing is full of words that sound good and mean little. Sustainable, ethical, handmade and locally made are all claims a customer may rely on, and some are regulated. We only use them when you can show what stands behind them: the mill, the workshop, the certificate. Fiber content, care instructions and country of origin are stated as they are.

We do not use a photograph or a likeness without the right to use it, and we keep a simple record of who shot what and how it may be used.

How we work inside your team

One person approves what goes out, usually the founder. We ask for about an hour a week and we plan around your production crunch, doing the heavier thinking in the weeks when you have room. Your website, your accounts, your images and your customer list stay yours.

Questions we hear

Are you a fashion PR agency or a showroom?

Neither. We are a business management firm. We handle the business side of the label, and marketing, PR and social media are part of that. We do not represent your line to buyers on commission.

Can you guarantee press or stockists?

No. Editors and buyers make their own decisions. We promise the agreed work, on schedule, and an honest report on what it is doing.

We are a two-person label. Are we too small?

Probably not. The smaller the team, the more each hour of the founder’s time is worth. A conversation will tell us both quickly.

Who owns the images and the copy?

You do, under the agreed payment terms. Your site, your accounts and your contact list are yours from the first day.
