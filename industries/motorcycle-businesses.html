Motorcycle dealers, shops and builders

A business manager for the motorcycle shop that is too busy in season to market itself

When the weather turns, a motorcycle business is flat out. Sales floor, service bays, parts counter, all at once. That is exactly when riders are looking for you, and exactly when nobody has an hour for the website. We do that work as part of your crew.

A season that does not wait

A motorcycle business lives by the calendar. In the cold months there is time and not much traffic. In the warm months there is traffic and no time. Most shops plan to catch up on marketing in the off season, and most off seasons disappear into inventory, repairs to the building and a well-earned rest.

So the pattern repeats. The first warm weekend arrives, riders start searching for a service slot or a used bike, and the shop’s website still shows last year’s open house. The social accounts went quiet in October. The reviews from a busy summer were never answered.

Riders notice. This is a community that talks, in parking lots and group chats, and a shop’s reputation travels on what people can see as well as what they hear.

What we take off the owner’s desk

As your outsourced business manager we carry the recurring work around the shop, on a calendar that is set before the season starts. The list is agreed in writing. It can include:

  • A weekly check-in with the owner or general manager, kept short, with decisions written down.
  • A seasonal plan built in the quiet months, so spring content, service reminders and event dates are ready before the doors get busy.
  • Follow-up routines for enquiries, quotes and service reminders, so a lead from Saturday is not found in a notebook two weeks later.
  • Coordination with the people who look after your website, your listings and your print.
  • A monthly report in plain words on what went out and what came back.

We do not run your service department or your books. We work alongside your service manager, your accountant and your manufacturer representative, and we make sure the things that involve all three do not fall between them.

Marketing: three counters, one story

Most motorcycle businesses are three businesses under one roof. Sales, service, and parts and apparel each have their own customer and their own reason to visit. Marketing that only talks about bikes for sale leaves two of the three counters out.

We begin with the same three questions we ask every client. Who are you? What are you about? Why should a rider come to you and not the shop across town or the website with free shipping? A shop that can answer in plain words has most of its marketing written already.

Then we work through what a rider finds:

  • The website. Hours, address, phone and a way to book service, all visible on a phone at the top of the page. Current inventory if you sell. A plain page for each service you offer, with what it involves and how long it takes.
  • Local listings. The details that decide whether you appear when someone searches for motorcycle service nearby. They need to match everywhere.
  • Reviews. A simple routine for asking after a sale or a service, and replies drafted for your approval. A calm answer to a bad review often wins more trust than ten good ones.
  • Email and text reminders. Service intervals, seasonal checks, storage and battery care. Useful messages, sent sparingly.

PR: events, builds and the local riding community

A motorcycle shop has real stories. A demo day. A charity ride. A custom build that took a winter. A technician who earned a new certification. A twentieth anniversary. A new line arriving.

PR for a shop means telling those stories to the people who would care: local news, regional riding publications, clubs, event calendars and community groups. We write the announcement, gather the photographs, build the short list of contacts and follow up. For a custom builder, we prepare the build story, the specification sheet and the images an editor needs, so a feature is easy for them to say yes to.

We cannot promise coverage. That decision belongs to the editor. We can make sure the story is told well and arrives on time.

Social media: the shop floor is the content

A motorcycle business has an advantage most firms would envy. The work is visual, the customers are proud of what they ride, and something worth photographing happens every day.

What is usually missing is a person whose job it is. We set up a routine your crew can keep:

  • A shared folder where anyone on the floor can drop a photo or a short clip from their phone.
  • A monthly calendar you approve once, mixing new arrivals, finished service work, staff, customer bikes shared with permission, and upcoming rides.
  • Captions written in the shop’s own voice. Plain, knowledgeable, never salesy.
  • Replies and messages watched, so a question about a part or a service slot gets an answer the same day.

We would rather you posted three times a week all year than daily in May and never in November. Riders who see a shop that stays in touch through the winter remember it in the spring.

Brand rules, finance offers and honest claims

Dealers work within their manufacturers’ rules. Logos, model names, advertised prices and cooperative advertising programs all come with conditions. We read the brand guidelines before we write, and anything that carries a manufacturer’s mark goes through your usual approval.

Finance and price advertising has its own rules. An advertised monthly payment or rate generally has to be shown with the terms behind it, and the requirements differ from state to state. We keep offers plain and complete, and we ask you to confirm the wording with the lender or your counsel before it runs.

We do not write performance or safety claims that the shop cannot support, and we do not post a customer’s bike or face without their say.

How we work inside your team

One person approves what goes out. In a shop that is usually the owner or the general manager. We ask for about an hour a week in season, and we do the heavier planning with you in the quiet months when you have room to think.

Your website, your listings, your accounts and your customer list remain yours. We work inside them with access you control.

Where to start

The best time to start is before the season. The 90-Day Plan fits a winter or early spring well: a review of your website and social accounts, your three answers, five rewritten pages, twelve pieces of content and a monthly discussion with your team, for one fixed fee. You open the season with the shop looking the way it deserves to.

If the season has already started, we begin with the few things that matter most this month and build from there. Tell us about the shop and where the work is getting stuck.

Questions we hear

Do you only work with dealerships?

No. We work with franchised dealers, independent service shops and custom builders. The mix of sales, service and parts is different in each, and so is the plan.

Our crew has no time to take photos. Will this still work?

Yes. We keep the ask small: a shared folder and a few phone photos a week. We do the selecting, the writing and the scheduling.

Can you manage our manufacturer co-op advertising?

We can prepare material that follows the program’s rules and keep the paperwork organized. Claims and reimbursements stay with you and your manufacturer representative.

Will you promise more sales this season?

No. Nobody can honestly promise that. We promise the agreed work on schedule and a plain report on what it is doing for the shop.
